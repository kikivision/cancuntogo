Why we picked it

If you rank Cancún resorts purely by how much water there is to play in, this one wins and it isn't close. Moon Palace The Grand has an actual waterpark — slides, a lazy river, a wave pool, splash pads for the little ones — on top of nine pools in The Grand section alone. Nothing else on the strip gives a family that much water without buying a separate day pass.

And it still does the thing this site cares most about: Grand Swim-Up Suites, where your terrace opens straight into the pool. They're big (around 795 sq ft), sleep four, and come with the Palace signature double whirlpool tub inside. A swim-up suite that a family of four can actually share is rare — that's the reason this mega-resort earns a spot on a list otherwise full of adults-only boutiques.

Booking The Grand also buys you the run of the whole Moon Palace complex — the Sunrise and Nizuc sections, including the FlowRider® Double surf simulator and the big Wired arcade over at Sunrise. It doesn't work the other way around: Sunrise and Nizuc guests can't wander into The Grand.

The water park at Moon Palace: what's included, and who gets in

This is the thing people ask about Moon Palace more than anything else, and the answer has a catch worth understanding before you pick a room. The Moon Palace complex is three resorts sharing one stretch of coast — Sunrise, Nizuc and The Grand — and they do not have equal run of each other's facilities.

Book The Grand and you get all of it. The waterpark, the nine pools in The Grand's own section, both wave simulators and the Wired arcade over at Sunrise — included, no day pass, no upcharge, no leaving the property. Book Sunrise or Nizuc and the reverse is not true: those guests can't come into The Grand. So if the water is the reason you're going, The Grand is the section that guarantees you the whole complex rather than part of it.

Worth putting in context: in the Hotel Zone, a water park is normally a separate outing you pay for and drive to. Here it's part of the all-inclusive. That's the single biggest practical difference between basing a family at Moon Palace and basing one on the strip.

The park is built as five separate zones, and the reason that matters is age spread: a seven-year-old and a thirteen-year-old are not looking for the same ride, and here they don't have to share one.

Water Slides

Six of them — four open body slides at a 48" minimum, and two inner-tube slides at 55". Lifeguards enforce the heights strictly and won't allow a child to ride on a parent's lap, so measure before you promise anyone anything.

Jungle Safari

The in-between zone, and the most useful one. Medium slides and a jungle-themed water play area for kids too big for the splash pad and too small for the main slides.

Lazy River

A wide, calm loop for kids and adults both — the stretch of the day that reliably works for everyone at once.

Wave Pool

Rolling surf in a controlled setting. On this coast that's not a gimmick: when sargassum is in, this is the ocean swim you were hoping for.

Splash Zone

Water jets, sprays and toddler-sized mini slides, shallow and set apart — no height minimum, so the ones turned away from the big slides still have somewhere of their own.

There are two wave simulators at Moon Palace, and they are not the same ride. This trips people up constantly, so it's worth being precise. Sunrise has the original FlowRider® Double — two lanes, the standard model. The Grand's water park has the upgraded ProFlow® Triple Wave Simulator — three lanes, so the queue moves faster and you get more rides out of an afternoon.

Which matters because of the access rule above: book The Grand and you can ride both. Book Sunrise and the Double is the only one you get. The ProFlow runs 9:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. and is free in the general rotation; private slots for up to eight people are bookable at extra cost — the one genuine upcharge on an otherwise all-in property.

One more worth knowing: The Playroom, the staffed kids' club, is open 9 a.m. to 10 p.m. That's long enough that a dinner without children is actually possible — the part most families don't realize they're buying.

Where it sits on the Cancún coast

Carr. Cancún–
Chetumal

One thing to be straight about: Moon Palace The Grand is not in the Hotel Zone. It sits on the Carretera Cancún–Chetumal at Km 340, south of Punta Nizuc and well past where Blvd. Kukulcán ends — so the km markers used everywhere else on this site don't apply to it. It's a self-contained resort city on its own stretch of coast, not a link in the strip.

Practically, that means nothing is walkable and nothing is a short hop: Punta Cancún and the Hotel Zone's restaurants, malls and nightlife are a 20–30 minute drive north. The airport, on the other hand, is close — about 15 minutes. If you intend to leave the property regularly, this is the wrong base; if you intend to never leave it, the isolation is the product.

Sargassum exposure

High

This coast south of Punta Nizuc faces the open Caribbean with no shelter at all, and it sits on the same exposed run as the southern Hotel Zone and the Riviera Maya beaches beyond it. In the April–August peak, sargassum here is a when, not an if — this is among the more exposed positions of any resort we cover.

Moon Palace is, however, the one property where that matters least. The whole proposition is the water footprint inside the resort — a full waterpark, nine pools, swim-up suites. Guests who book here for the beach are booking the wrong thing; guests who book it for everything else will barely register a bad sargassum week.

Who it's for

✓ Stay here if…

  • You want the most water per dollar in Cancún — a full waterpark plus nine pools, all included.
  • You're traveling multigen and the group wants very different things from a vacation.
  • You want a swim-up suite the whole family can share, not an adults-only one.
  • A short transfer matters — it's one of the closest big resorts to the airport, roughly 15 minutes.

✕ Skip it if…

  • You want to walk out to bars and restaurants — this sits south of the Hotel Zone, so it's a 20–30 minute ride each way.
  • You want intimate. The Grand alone is 1,300+ rooms, and getting anywhere means a long walk or a shuttle.
  • You're traveling July–October and a clean beach is non-negotiable — sargassum hits this stretch. Read our sargassum season guide →
  • Hard-sell membership presentations would ruin your week — they're persistent at Palace properties.
